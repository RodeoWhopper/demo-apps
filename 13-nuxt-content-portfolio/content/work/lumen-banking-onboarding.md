## Context

Opening a business account at Lumen required 22 screens, three PDF uploads and a callback from a human. Sixty-nine percent of founders who started never finished.

## What we learned

Twelve interviews with founders who had abandoned the flow gave us a simple pattern: they didn't have the information to hand. Registration numbers, director IDs, proof of address — the form asked for everything up front, in the order the compliance team liked, not the order the founder could answer.

## Principles

1. **Ask for what people have in their pocket first.** Photograph an ID, we prefill the rest.
2. **Never block on a missing document.** Let people continue and come back.
3. **Show progress honestly.** Nine steps, always visible, always the same nine.

## Design

The new flow leads with document capture. A photo of the company registration extracts the legal name, number and address; the founder confirms rather than types. Compliance checks run in the background while the founder continues, and any step can be parked with a single "I'll add this later" link.

> "It felt like the bank had already done its homework." — pilot participant

## Results

The pilot ran with 800 applicants in Q3 2024. Completion doubled, and — the number the compliance team cared about — manual review time per application dropped by a third because data arrived structured instead of as PDFs.
